Mental Health, Workplace Wellbeing and Access to Support¶

Business Context:¶

Organisations need to understand factors associated with mental-health wellbeing and treatment engagement because mental-health issues can affect employees' wellbeing, workplace experience and ability to engage with work. The purpose of this project is to explore patterns between demographic, workplace, lifestyle and mental-health-related variables and identify relationships that may be useful for organisational decision-making.

Dataset:¶

The dataset(Alam Shihab, 2025) contains information including Gender, Country, Occupation, SelfEmployed, FamilyHistory, DaysIndoors, Treatment, HabitsChange, MentalHealthHistory, Increasing Stress, MoodSwings, SocialWeakness, CopingStruggles, WorkInterest, SocialWeakness, MentalHealthInterview, CareOptions. This data will help us in finding patterns and possible solutions.

Potential Decisions:¶

As our audience is organisations so we can let them know after analysis:

Where wellbeing support may need more attention

Whether different groups show different patters

Whether workplace or lifestyle affects mental health

How different age groups respond to treatment

family_treatment = pd.crosstab(
    healthcare['FamilyHistory'],
    healthcare['Treatment'],
    normalize='index'
) * 100

    kind='bar', stacked=True, figsize=(7,5)
)

plt.title('Treatment Status by Family History')
plt.xlabel('Family History')
plt.ylabel('Percentage of Respondents')
plt.legend(title='Treatment')
plt.show()

The chart shows that the patients with family history have a higher treatment proportion. This finding helps the organisation to understand that employee mental health can differ according to family background. For technical concepts, the crosstab() function in pandas compares the 2 columns of the same table or dataset. Then, I have used simple bar chart to show how family history can affect on getting treatment.

Strengths and Limitations:¶

Strengths includes large clean dataset with almost 82000 entries. Missing, duplicated and invalid values were addressed. Dataset had multiple variables to explore the different aspect of mental health, workplace wellbeing and care options availibility. While on the other there were some limitation in data analysis most importantly the dataset does not represent wider population from a lot of countries. Some relation were weak and could not help in extracting a meaningful insights. The dataset has uneven country representation which hinders in finding relations on country level.

Implications:¶

  1. Family background showed a notable difference in treat rates.
  2. Treatment rates varied by gender.
  3. Different occupation groups showed different rates of increasing stress.
  4. Long indoor periods mostly associated with more coping struggles.
  5. Gender groups had high fluctuations in response to mental health interviews.
  6. Many respondents either did not know or were not sure about the availabe mental health care options.
  7. The dataset consists of few countries where almost half of the respondents were from only top 3 countries.

Data Driven Recommendations:¶

  1. Improvement of awareness of available support is necessary because even if a lot of support is availabe but people do not even know about so the support has not use.
  2. As lot people declined participating in mental health interviews so confidential support channels are very good option.
  3. In business and corporate occupation people experienced increasing stress so workplace wellbeing should be implemented.
  4. Family history affect treats so early support is a good advantage in getting good results.
  5. Data was not collected evenly and widely so more data can help in making decision on country level or even city levels.
